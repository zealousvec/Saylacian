# Old Nubian reading scope

Saylacian provides limited character transliteration between selected Coptic-derived Old Nubian characters and scholarly Latin readings. It does not translate meaning, provide a complete alphabet, interpret manuscripts, or support modern Nubian varieties such as Nobiin, Kenzi/Mattokki, Dongolawi/Andaandi or Midob.

The current table contains 29 character readings from the peer-reviewed 2024 *UCLA Encyclopedia of Egyptology* overview. Its **Table 1** lists each character's scholarly Latin transliteration separately from its phonetic values; the letter reference displays both fields. Unknown characters are preserved with a warning. Reverse Latin-to-script conversion can be ambiguous, especially for `ou`, which can represent `ⲟⲩ` or `ⳣ`. Saylacian preserves combining marks and shows cited phonetic alternatives for `ⲏ`, `ⲩ`, `ⲉⲓ` and `ⲟⲩ`. For a supralinear stroke directly over `l`, `n`, `r` or `j`, it offers an `i`-before-letter alternative. If a stroke is over a vowel immediately after one of those consonants, it offers `i` before that consonant; the 2024 overview's example `ⲟⲕⲧⲁⲕⲛⲁ̄` is rendered by the app's character table as `oktaknā`, with `oktakinā` among the alternatives. Other vowel contexts are preserved without an inferred `i` insertion. A supralinear stroke can also mark a numeral, which Saylacian does not convert. Alternative lists are capped at 12. These readings do not interpret manuscripts or resolve differences between editions. A fluent Old Nubian specialist has not reviewed the implementation.

The table data are adapted from Vincent W. J. van Gerven Oei, [“Old Nubian,” *UCLA Encyclopedia of Egyptology* (2024)](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/03x6d68h), [Table 1 PDF](https://escholarship.org/content/qt03x6d68h/qt03x6d68h_noSplash_40a6faae9729137f4f62f11687cae5cf.pdf), DOI [10.5070/G9.3919](https://doi.org/10.5070/G9.3919), under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The article identifies 29 characters and two digraphs. For reverse readings with many ambiguities, Saylacian prioritizes single-token alternatives in input order before combined alternatives. The list remains capped at 12, so later choices can still be omitted and the app warns when this happens. Attribution and modification details are in [third-party notices](../THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md). The bundled table no longer uses the previous DBMNT-derived mapping; its table-redistribution blocker is resolved. Specialist review remains outstanding. Saylacian does not include manuscript passages or a word lexicon from the separately licensed Old Nubian edition. Any future use of DBMNT material still needs written permission.

Numeral limitation sources: Eugenia B. Smagina, *The Old Nubian Language*, translated by José Andrés Alonso de la Fuente (punctum books, 2017), §37, describes Old Nubian numbers written with alphabetic numerical signs and a diacritic. Gerald M. Browne, *Old Nubian Grammar* (Lincom Europa, 2002), §1.2.3, describes numerical signs bearing supraliteral strokes. Saylacian uses these references only to explain a limitation; it does not reproduce their text or numeral mappings. Smagina's book is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 and is not a source for new bundled data.
